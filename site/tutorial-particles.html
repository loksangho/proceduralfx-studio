Particles with a POP Network

Tutorial 2 · about 10 minutes

A POP Network is a particle simulation built from nodes, as in Houdini. Inside it, a POP Source gives birth to particles, a POP Solver moves them each frame, and forces such as gravity and wind push them. You'll build a spark fountain like this one:

1. Something to emit from

  1. At the top of the network editor (the OBJ level), press Tab, type geometry and press Enter. A new geometry object appears.
  2. Double-click it to go inside, then press Tab and add a sphere. Particles will come from its surface.
  3. In the sphere's parameters, make Radius small, say 0.2.

2. Add the POP Network

  1. Press Tab, type popnet and add the POP Network.
  2. Drag from the sphere's output to the POP Network's first input. The network shows its particles straight away.
  3. Press Space. Particles pour out of the sphere and fall under gravity.

3. Look inside

Double-click the POP Network. A new one comes with four nodes:

  • POP Object: the particles from the last frame, going into the solver's first input.
  • POP Source: births new particles, in the solver's third input (post-solve), so they first move on the next frame.
  • POP Solver: moves the particles, ages them, and removes those past their life.
  • Gravity Force: after the solver, with the display flag, so it's what you see.

Click POP Source and try its settings:

  • Birth rate (per second): how many particles are born.
  • Life expectancy and Life variance: how long they live, in seconds.
  • Initial velocity and Velocity variance: set the y of Initial velocity to 4 to throw them upwards, and raise the variance to spread them out.

4. Add wind and colour

  1. Press Tab and add a POP Wind. Wire Gravity Force's output into it, then double-click POP Wind to make it the display node. Set Wind velocity x to 2 to blow the sparks sideways. Air resistance sets how quickly they catch the wind.
  2. Add a POP Color after POP Wind and double-click it. Set Color to Ramp over life. Choose yellow for Color (at birth) and dark red for Color at the end of life.

Forces and colours can go before the solver too, in its second input (pre-solve). Every POP after the solver runs each frame on what it outputs.

5. More to try

  • POP Drag slows particles down. POP Speed Limit caps their speed.
  • POP Kill removes particles by an expression, such as @P.y < -1, or inside a box.
  • Ground Plane makes particles bounce off the floor. Merge it with the POP Object into the solver's first input.
  • POP Wrangle runs your own VEX on every particle, for example v@force += set(0, 2, 0);.

6. Use the particles

Press U to go back up. The POP Network outputs points carrying v, age, life, id and their colour. Export them as a sprite sheet straight away (see tutorial 1), or feed them into Copy to Points to put a shape on every particle, or into Particle Fluid Surface to turn them into a liquid.
